MscL Gating Simulation.

1. Background

In aQtSOME, GUVs that reach the vicinity of a tumor boundary are squeezed by the surrounding tissue, and the resulting membrane tension opens mechanosensitive (MS) channels in their membranes. Because membrane tension cannot be measured inside living tissue, we built a physical model of the whole process and tested two questions:

  1. Does the squeezing raise the membrane tension to the value needed to open the channels?
  2. Do the channels open before the membrane ruptures?

Both hold, even under the conservative condition that the membrane must withstand the tension for several hours. All results can be reproduced with the simulator in Section 4.

Channel assumed: MscL Q56P. Q56P is a mutant of MscL with a single amino-acid substitution, and it opens at two-thirds of the wild-type tension (Blount et al., 1996). The wild-type opening tension (11.8 pN/nm) exceeds the tension a membrane can withstand for long periods, so this simulation assumes Q56P. All results below refer to Q56P.

2. Model overview

Every link from stress to shape to tension is monotonic, so the harder the squeezing, the higher the tension. Therefore, if τopen<τlys, the channels open first. The stress at the tumor boundary far exceeds the value needed for opening and decreases continuously with distance from the boundary. A GUV approaching the tumor therefore always passes through a range of Δσ in which it opens and does not rupture. We call this range the opening window.

3. Calculation of each stage

3.1 Tumor growth creates stress in the surrounding tissue

The stress values were read from Fig. 1B of Stylianopoulos (2017), which shows the predictions of a finite-element model of tumor growth (Stylianopoulos et al., 2013). G and ν are also taken from that model.

Time pointPeak circumferential stressRadial stress at the same positionΔσ
Day 2about +15 kPaabout −24 kPaabout 40 kPa
Day 6about +33 kPaabout −28 kPaabout 60 kPa
Day 8about +66 kPaabout −30 kPaabout 96 kPa

Δσ falls to a few kPa within about 0.5–2 mm into the normal tissue. This distance is far longer than a GUV (about 5 µm), so the tissue around a GUV can be treated as uniformly stressed.

With its liquid interior, a GUV cannot resist the squeezing and deforms immediately according to the Δσ where it arrives. We therefore take the moment of arrival as the spherical reference state.

3.2 The stress flattens the GUV

Style, Wettlaufer and Dufresne (2015) extended Eshelby's (1957) theory of ellipsoidal regions in elastic solids to liquid droplets with surface tension. Applying their solution to our loading gives:

ln⁡k≈15(1−ν)Δσ2(7−5ν)G+(17−19ν)τR

The second term in the denominator is the membrane's resistance. The equation reduces to Eshelby's classical result for τ→0 and to Eq. 7 of Style et al. for ν=1/2, and it agrees with their general solution to machine precision. Because the tension of a fluid membrane is the same everywhere, the equilibrium shape is obtained by inserting the current τ, even though the tension rises as the GUV deforms.

The τ in the denominator is set by the deformation, and the deformation is set by τ, so shape and tension are determined together. We therefore calculate backward, starting from the tension (Fig. 6).

This type of theory has been shown to match measured droplet shapes up to far-field strains of 42% (Style et al., 2015, Nat Phys). In our calculation, opening and rupture occur at far-field strains of about 0.18–0.22, within that range.

3.3 The added membrane area creates membrane tension

We calculate α exactly from the surface area of an oblate spheroid at constant volume and convert it to tension with the relation measured by Evans and Rawicz (1990):

α=kBT8πκln⁡(1+cτA/κ1+cτ0A/κ)+τ−τ0KA,c=124π

Here A is the membrane area, kBT is the thermal energy and c is a constant for quasi-spherical vesicles. The first term is the contribution of the undulations and the second that of stretching. Reaching 5 pN/nm requires a total area increase of 4.1%, compared with 2.5% from stretching alone. Including the undulations increases the deformation and stress required, so this term works against opening, not for it. The membrane is assumed to consist of DOPC only (KA = 200 pN/nm).

3.4 Does tension reach opening or rupture first?

A GUV approaching the tumor is exposed for a long time to a slowly rising tension. We therefore set τlys to 5 pN/nm, the tension a membrane can withstand for several hours.

The curves in Fig. 11 were calculated with the two-state Boltzmann equation routinely used in patch-clamp experiments:

Popen(τ)=11+exp⁡[−ΔA(τ−T1/2)kBT],Pany(τ)=1−(1−Popen(τ))N

Here ΔA is the change in membrane area on opening, which sets how steeply the curve rises (6.5 nm², the wild-type value from Sukharev et al., 1999).

In this simulation, a GUV is defined as "open" when any one of its channels is open. Because GUVs can be expected to be administered in large numbers rather than singly, we use as the opening criterion the point at which the probability that at least one channel in a GUV is open reaches 50%. Rupture is judged by whether the tension reaches τlys. MscS was excluded because it inactivates under slowly rising tension (Akitake et al., 2005).

5. Results

As Δσ increases, the curve in Fig. 12 first crosses the opening-tension line and then the lysis-tension line. The key values are listed below.

An opening window exists at every diameter from 1 to 20 µm. Smaller GUVs have a wider window that is shifted to higher stress; the stress required is at most about 9 kPa.

An opening window appears at about 58 channels or more, and the more channels, the further the window extends toward lower stress.

6. Discussion

6.1 What the results mean

Opening precedes rupture. The effective opening tension of a GUV with 1,000 Q56P channels (3.1 pN/nm) is below the lysis tension the membrane withstands for hours (5 pN/nm). The relation holds even with this long-holding value rather than the lysis tension from short tests (8–10 pN/nm).

A GUV does not need to stay inside the opening window. Fig. 15 shows how a GUV approaches the tumor.

Before opening (①), the tension is below the lysis tension, so the membrane does not rupture however long the approach takes. When the tension reaches the opening tension (②), the channels open immediately. The width of the opening window shows that point ② lies before rupture; it is not a range in which the GUV has to stay.

The conclusion is robust to uncertainties in tissue mechanics. The interface stress is 9–22 times the stress needed to open. Uncertainties in tissue stiffness, Poisson's ratio, the stiffness of the GUV's contents and the lipid composition all shift the opening and rupture points in the same direction, and their effects are smaller than this margin (Appendix A12). The condition is also satisfied over a wide range: diameters of 1–20 µm and 58 or more channels.

6.2 Proposals for the aQtSOME design

  • Channel: use an MscL mutant with a lowered opening tension, such as Q56P. When long-lasting loading is assumed, a low opening tension decides whether the condition is met. Do not use MscS, which inactivates under slow loading.
  • Number of channels: 60 or more per GUV. With 1,000, the margin is about 17 times the minimum, enough to absorb variation in reconstitution efficiency.
  • GUV diameter: any diameter from 1 to 20 µm works. The current 5 µm is sufficient; making GUVs smaller widens the window but raises the stress needed to open.
  • Preparation: match the osmolarity inside and outside the GUVs precisely. An imbalance of only about 1.5 mM (0.5%) inflates a 5 µm GUV to a tension of 5 pN/nm by itself.
