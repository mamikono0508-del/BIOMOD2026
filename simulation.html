Simulation.

Testing molecular behavior through physical models.

Two complementary simulations examine the key requirements of our proposed mechanoresponsive system: the local spread of calcium ions and the membrane tension needed to activate MscL.

REACTION–DIFFUSION MODEL

Ca²⁺ Diffusion Simulation

We used a reaction–diffusion model to calculate how far Ca²⁺ entering through MscL spreads inside a GUV, comparing free diffusion with systems containing EGTA or BAPTA. Under free diffusion, the region where Ca²⁺ reached the level needed to trigger actin polymerization was limited to within a few micrometers of the channel. Adding a chelator narrowed this region to the immediate vicinity of the channel (about 10 nm) and kept it stable over time.

MECHANICAL GATING MODEL

MscL Gating Simulation

We built a physical model of a GUV squeezed by the tissue around a tumor. The model links tissue stress, GUV deformation and membrane tension, and tests whether the mechanosensitive channel MscL opens before the membrane ruptures. For a 5 µm GUV with 1,000 MscL Q56P channels, the channels opened at a membrane tension of 3.1 pN/nm, below the 5 pN/nm that the membrane can withstand for hours. There was a range of tissue stress in which the channels open without rupture. The stress at the tumor boundary was 9–22 times higher than the stress needed for opening. This held for GUV diameters of 1–20 µm and for about 60 or more channels per GUV.
